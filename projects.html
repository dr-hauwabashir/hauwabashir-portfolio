Projects

Campaigns at the Women Empowerment Centre

I founded the Women Empowerment Centre at Cyprus International University and coordinated it from 2020 to 2025, with a team of students. Prof. Majid Hashemipour, then Senior Advisor to the Board of Trustees, helped see it through to its establishment. We didn’t work from a standard idea of what empowerment means. We started from the subjects women are taught not to talk about, and from the fact that each woman meets them differently.

Women in Pursuit of Knowledge

The centre began with this seminar. I organised it for women in northern Nigeria, where many women are still kept from basic education. It was meant to happen in person, but COVID-19 moved it online, and that turned out to help: more than 100 women joined from the safety of their homes.

Late 2021 – 2022Founder and coordinator

Education for young Afghan women

After the Taliban took over Afghanistan in 2021, many young women there lost the chance to keep studying. Between the end of 2021 and 2022, as they looked for somewhere to continue their education, we enrolled more than 45 of them at Cyprus International University. It was the centre’s first large project. Many of these women have since graduated.

The university reported on the centre and its scholarships for Afghan girls in March 2022: “CIU embarked on a project to create a sustainable future for girls.”

December 2022Founder and coordinatorWith VOIS Cyprus

Orange the World: a silent walk against gender-based violence

The problem

Violence against women continues because people stay quiet about it, on campus as much as anywhere. For the global 16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence, I wanted our campaign to make that silence visible.

What we did

Under the UN theme “UNiTE! Activism to end violence against women and girls,” we ran a programme across the 16 Days:

  • 5 DecemberAwareness walk, setting off from the Çevik Uraz Center at 12:30. More than 500 students, women and men, walked across campus in orange, carrying placards and covering their mouths to stand for the silence around violence against women.
  • 6 DecemberA mixed-gender football match at CIU Arena.
  • 9 DecemberSeminar, “Set the Stage: A Dialogue on Gender-Based Violence,” at the Çevik Uraz Center Conference Hall.
  • OnlineAn Instagram series explaining what gender-based violence is and how it affects women’s mental health, ending on Human Rights Day, 10 December.
  • On campusQR codes linking to an information document on what gender-based violence is, how to protect yourself and how to respond.

We published every poster in English and Turkish.

Three posts from the campaign’s Instagram series.

What happened

More than 500 students joined the walk. Afterwards, more women came to see the centre as a safe space, a place to talk about things they had always shied away from.

Silence has never protected a woman.
Hauwa Bashir Muhammad, on the 2022 silent walk

What I would do differently

I would build a proper structure around the centre from the start, so that what we built keeps going instead of coming to a pause when the people running it move on.

Go Green with Feminine Hygiene

The problem

Feminine hygiene is something people rarely talk about. Periods carry a stigma, and many young women feel deep shame about their menstrual cycle. Because nobody talks about it, few women ask what is in the products they use every month, or how those products may affect their health.

What we did

We ran a campaign on campus to “educate and sensitize young women about green options for feminine hygiene”: organic and reusable products women could choose with their health in mind. Talking openly about the products was also a way to take some of the shame out of the subject.

  • 31 October“Rating sanitary products from most to least harmful.” At our stand, visitors sorted cards for organic and inorganic tampons, disposable and reusable pads, and menstrual cups.
  • 1 NovemberA free skin care consultation with Dr. Mehrnoush Radmehr, medical doctor.
  • During the weekA session in the university’s anatomy lab, and a daily series of tips on Instagram.

What I would do differently

I would work with a local pharmacy or brand so students could try the organic and reusable products for themselves, and I would measure whether anyone actually switched. And I would keep the conversation going long after one week, until periods stop being a taboo on campus and no girl feels ashamed to say she is on her period. It is one of the most natural things there is.

2022Founder and coordinator

Women in STEM

Through Women in STEM, over 25 young women, mostly from developing countries, joined STEM programmes at CIU on scholarships, working toward a gender-just campus. The scholarships required them to keep a minimum CGPA, and more than 8 kept it above 3.50. We also placed them in hands-on training in the university’s engineering and sustainability workshops, using drill presses and laser cutters, and on 7 October 2022 we hosted an Instagram Live, “Being a Woman in Engineering,” with three young women engineers.

What I would do differently

Before anyone enrolled, I would run proper career counselling, so each woman chose a field that matched what she cares about. I would bring an engineering company in early, so the training connected to real work. And I would follow up with the women after their scholarships, to see where their studies took them.

March 2022 and March 2023Founder and coordinator

International Women’s Day: what womanhood means to us

The centre’s first public event was a celebration of International Women’s Day in March 2022.

In 2023 we asked a question instead of giving an answer: what does womanhood mean to you? At our stand, students tied purple ribbons on each other’s wrists and wrote on two boards, one asking them to describe womanhood in one word and one asking them to name an important woman in their life. Online, we ran the #wecwomanhood challenge, inviting people to post a 30-second video saying in one word what womanhood means to them. Every answer was different.

2020–2025Founder and coordinator

Mentorship, research circles and the Safe Space

Every semester I held mentorship sessions with the students, mostly one on one with me. We talked about their progress and their challenges, and it gave them a safe place to share their own ideas and the things they might be too shy to raise anywhere else.

We also paired students with women leaders who could guide them, people who had already made their way through the barriers our members were facing.

I also brought them into research. I grouped members into small teams and gave each team a topic close to their own lives, an issue that affects women in its own way. Each team then presented what it had found to the centre.

We also ran Safe Space sessions: small, informal circles where women could talk about what they usually keep to themselves. We don’t publish photos of these, to protect the women who took part.
