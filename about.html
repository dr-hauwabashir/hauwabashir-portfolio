About me

Good intentions are everywhere. I turn them into results for women.

To me, women’s empowerment is not one standard definition. It starts with understanding the specific challenges each woman meets, especially the ones nobody talks about.

I grew up in northern Nigeria, where many of the things that shape a woman’s life are not discussed openly, and where reaching a PhD is hard for a woman. I started my undergraduate degree in Nigeria at 15, in a different state from the one I was born in. The limits set for women like me were never my measure. Years later, I made my own way to Cyprus for my PhD. There I met women from very different countries carrying the same silence. I wanted them to find more in themselves too, especially the ones whose circumstances were like mine.

In early 2020 I organised an online seminar, Women in Pursuit of Knowledge, for women in northern Nigeria. It was planned for a hall, but COVID-19 moved it online, and more than 100 women joined from their homes.

So I founded the Women Empowerment Centre at Cyprus International University and ran it for five years. Most of the work came down to one thing: helping women know themselves better. For some, that meant talking openly about periods and breast cancer. For others, it meant marching in silence against gender-based violence. And for new students, it meant hands-on experience in their own fields, from engineering workshops to banking.

Before Cyprus, I had already seen what communication can do for women’s health. In 2018, for my master’s, my coursemates and I ran a campaign against maternal deaths in villages between Kaduna and Abuja, using storytelling, awareness campaigns and public relations to reach women in their own communities.

My PhD turned to the news. I read 467 articles from five Nigerian newspapers across the first year of COVID-19, to see whose voices were heard in a health crisis. Officials supplied most of the coverage. Ordinary people were talked about far more often than they were heard.

I speak Hausa and English, and work in Turkish.

How I work · The Triangle of Empowerment

Empowerment has no single shape. Neither does a woman.

I first shared this at TEDxCIU in 2024. Empowerment starts with the woman herself, grows through a community, and asks her to step outside her comfort zone. Each side holds up the other two.

  1. YouKnowing what drives you, building confidence and growing in self-awareness.
  2. CommunityStarting the conversation, and making the space safe enough to have it.
  3. Comfort zoneOpen-mindedness, and mentors who help you step beyond what feels familiar.
True equality remains elusive, perhaps unreachable. But it’s equity that might hold the key, ensuring women are heard, respected, and given room to exhibit their talents.
From my TEDxCIU talk, 2024
